# Product Challenge: JWT Login and Validation

- **“Same generic outcome” is not testable as written.** FR-004 covers unknown email, wrong password, blank, malformed, and otherwise invalid input, but neither the spec nor product handoff defines which observable properties must match. Different error values, HTTP mappings, logs, or materially different timing can enumerate accounts. Although transport belongs to TASK-010, the architect should define one public authentication error contract now, keep diagnostic causes internal and non-sensitive, and require the unknown-account path to perform comparable password-hash work. Exact constant time is not credible; test response shape/status equivalence and review the implementation for obvious timing shortcuts.

- **Time claims have a precision gap.** “Exactly 15 minutes,” uniqueness within the same time unit, and rejection at the expiration instant conflict unless claim encoding and clock precision are fixed. JWT NumericDate commonly has second granularity; truncating issuance while deriving expiration from a higher-precision instant can shorten or lengthen observed life. Specify one injected UTC clock, NumericDate units and rounding, `exp = iat + 900`, and inclusive/exclusive checks. Select a skew value (including zero) and test both sides of the `iat`/`nbf` allowance; never apply it to `exp`, as FR-011 requires.

- **SC-005 is not reproducible.** “Normal local operating conditions” does not define hardware, sample size, concurrency, database state, warm-up, or percentile calculation. A one-second threshold may hide password-hash regressions or produce flaky CI. Treat it as a documented local benchmark with fixed fixtures, warm-up, sample count, and p95 calculation; do not make environment-dependent timing a brittle correctness gate.

- **SC-006 exceeds controllable scope.** “Inspection” of all logs, telemetry, test failures, and account representations has no bounded corpus, while FR-012 permits the token in one result. Define the application-owned outputs and sinks under review, use sentinel secrets/tokens, capture them in tests, and assert absence outside the explicit success field. Ensure exception inspection does not retain raw inputs.

- **Validation semantics after account change are implicit.** FR-009 returns the verified subject without requiring an account lookup, while revocation and account-status policy are explicitly out of scope and no such status exists in product truth. Preserve standalone cryptographic validation and do not silently add per-validation database existence checks; document that already-issued tokens remain valid until expiration. A different rule would introduce undeclared revocation behavior.

- **Security is intentionally incomplete.** A public credential-login surface without throttling/lockout remains susceptible to online guessing, even with a 15-minute token. Respect FR-015 rather than expanding this task, but flag the risk for the route-protection/integration backlog and avoid presenting CP1 authentication as production-hardened.

No `backlog/feedback/TASK-009.md` exists, so there is no current human feedback overriding these recommendations. CP1 requires JWT but adds no further acceptance detail.
