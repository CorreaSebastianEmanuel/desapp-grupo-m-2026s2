# Product challenge — TASK-018

- **Equivalence needs separate definitions before architecture.** FR-010 includes retrieval instant and attribution in delivery identity, while story 2.2 permits a later identical observation and FR-011 permits equivalent concurrent deliveries to converge. Equal football facts alone therefore cannot establish replay or convergence. Define exact delivery equivalence separately from unchanged catalog facts; resolve result-local relationships to qualified source identities before comparison. Test reordered facts, altered local references, later timestamps, fixture/live attribution changes and replacement-provider bindings independently. Recommend converging only equivalent deliveries; differing deliveries require a fresh attempt under FR-011. Converging merely identical catalog facts would lose observation lineage.

- **Concurrency examples must include an absent scope and shared canonical records.** FR-011 requires a revision observed before retrieval, but first imports have no accepted revision. FR-002 also permits concurrent scopes to create a shared league. Story 2.6 does not explicitly exercise either boundary. Define an absent-scope revision and demonstrate two competing first imports, plus simultaneous imports of different seasons for the same missing league. Recommend PostgreSQL uniqueness and transaction coordination with independently expected outcomes. A scheduler lock alone cannot satisfy domain idempotency; a global import lock is simpler but unnecessarily couples unrelated scopes. This is an internal planning choice, not a product escalation.

- **Final-state uniqueness can be confused with intermediate write conflicts.** FR-007 permits bound teams to change names/codes when TASK-005 uniqueness is preserved. Two teams exchanging unique codes can have a valid final catalog yet fail under sequential updates; similarly, a transferred bound player and a new same-name player must not be reconciled by processing order. Require explicit fixtures for these permutations and final-state validation of the whole candidate catalog. Recommend preserving bound identities and rejecting genuine final collisions. Do not silently reject a valid final state merely because the chosen write order encounters a temporary collision.

- **Observation time has an operational recovery limitation.** FR-012 and the assumptions deliberately trust retrieval instants, not source chronology. A future-dated accepted observation can make subsequent genuine retrievals stale; switching provider does not inherently establish comparable clocks. Document this limitation in the architecture and test it without adding timestamp clamping, clock tolerance or provider-specific ordering. Recommend retaining the specified ordering and safe stale outcome; automatic clock correction would introduce a new business rule. Any future recovery mechanism must be separately specified rather than hidden in reconciliation.
