# Product challenge — TASK-064

1. **Failure-budget scope can prematurely block unrelated checks.** Story 3.3 and FR-010 say two failures for unchanged inputs “in a stage,” without identifying whether failures aggregate across different checks. Two distinct first-time failures could exhaust a stage budget even though neither check was retried. Recommendation: architecture should define the counter by stage, check identity and relevant bound inputs; persist it across resume, distinguish interruption from completed failure, and test that unrelated file edits cannot reset it. A stage-wide budget is simpler but would obstruct diagnosis of independent failures without reducing repeated execution.

2. **Resume adoption needs an explicit attempt boundary.** Story 6.5 and FR-020 preserve saved settings while allowing adoption for future attempts; neither precisely addresses an interrupted agent session that is itself resumable. Applying new settings to that session risks changing identity within one attempt or relabeling its provenance. Recommendation: normal resume retains the saved assignment; explicit adoption starts a separately identified future attempt/session for affected stages and preserves the interrupted attempt. Test partial legacy settings, adoption after completed stages, and repository changes between resolution and launch. Missing legacy fields must require explicit selection where launch depends on them. This is a reversible architecture decision under the documented stability rule.

3. **Efficiency evidence could accidentally become circular or overly broad.** FR-007/008 require a versioned summary bound to source and canonical inputs, while FR-011/012 preserve unaffected evidence and require fresh independent gates. If generated summaries or subsequent QA/review reports are indiscriminately included in every input binding, producing the required evidence can invalidate its own checks; binding too little permits stale acceptance. Recommendation: define relevant input sets per check, keep generated results out of their own source binding, and record how final summary/verdict provenance connects to the checked revision. Test both report-only changes and changes to CI, verification obligations, source and model assignment. Preserve completed evidence as history without treating it as current proof after affected inputs change.

4. **Launcher fixtures must test failure propagation, not merely selected strings.** Story 6.4, FR-019/021 and the product handoff require unsupported-selection and availability failures. Fixtures that only inspect resolver output cannot prove that child launch rejection leaves the workflow unsuccessful or avoids fallback. Recommendation: use a deterministic child-launch substitute that rejects selected settings, records every invocation and exercises start and resume through stage outcome handling. Assert exactly the intended selection, visible failed-attempt provenance and no alternate launch. Separate locally invalid configuration from provider rejection; no live availability probe is needed. The frozen active run remains evidence of compatibility only, not successful execution of the new assignments.
